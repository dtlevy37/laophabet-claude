## 1. Verdict
- **The idea holds, but the payoff is moderate, and nearly all of it is available from lesson 20 on.** Lessons 1–19
  already follow it (planned backwards from sentences, 1.8 symbols per lesson, 7 of 19 lessons with 0–1 new symbols).
- **The window is now.** Most of the owner's words (ຮູ້ huu 'know', ໄດ້ dai 'can', ເຂົ້າໃຈ khao jai 'understand',
  ໝູ່ muu 'friend', ນ້ອງ nawng 'younger sibling') became readable only in lessons 15–19. Re-cutting 1–19 would move
  them earlier by only a few lessons, so it is not worth the cost.
- **Slowing symbols alone does not get a visitor talking.** The survival phrases that are still missing depend on a
  few specific symbols: ຂໍໂທດ khor thot 'sorry' and ເທົ່າໃດ thao dai 'how much' need ທ and final ◌ດ, prices need ພັນ
  phan 'thousand' (ພ, ◌ັ◌), and ແຊບ saep 'delicious' needs ຊ. The win is to **choose each symbol by the phrase it
  unlocks, and alternate it with word-only lessons** that use up the readable pool (about 45 verified words).
- **Recommended scope:** keep 1–19 as they are and re-plan lessons 20–33 as section S03 "Getting by". That is 14
  lessons with 10 symbols (0.7 per lesson), 7 of them word-only, and 3 conversations, ending with a full restaurant
  visit (order, "delicious", "how much?", pay). After S03, return to about 1 symbol per lesson. A permanent slowdown
  would push full literacy (about 40 role-units still to teach) back by roughly 30 lessons.

### 2b. Where it is heavy and where it stalls
- **Heavy:** 1–3 (unavoidable), 6 and 8 (3 symbols each), and **14–19: 11 symbols in 6 lessons**, the densest run
  since the start. The learner reaches lesson 20 just after that run, which is a good moment for word-only lessons.
- **Newest symbols have only one carrier word.** Core words using each symbol: ປ 1 (ໄປ pai 'go'), ໄ◌ 1, final ◌ບ 1
  (ຂອບໃຈ khop jai 'thanks'), ຢ 1 and ◌ູ 1 (ຢູ່ yuu 'be at'), ໂ◌ 1 (ໂຮງໝໍ hoong mor 'hospital'), final ◌ມ 0. A symbol
  met in one word is remembered as part of that word. The proposal gives each of these 2–4 more words (ໄດ້, ໄກ, ຮູ້,
  ໝູ່, ລູກ, ສິບ, ກີບ, ແຊບ, ຢາກ, ໂຮງຮຽນ, ອີ່ມ, ສາມ).
- **Meanings stall.** There are 42 core words but few verbs and no people, numbers, knowing or understanding.
  18 of the 21 sentences in 15–19 are built on ໄປ pai 'go' or ຢູ່ yuu 'be at'. There is still no way to say "I don't
  understand", "sorry", "how much?", "I'll pay", "my mother" or "two people".
- **Sentences rarely come back as lesson items.** L11–L19 recycle no earlier sentence; only the conversations do.
  Conversations that recombine old lines are the cheapest way to get repetition in real context.

### 2c. What the learning science implies for this course
1. **Decoding a new word from known letters is letter practice.** Each successful decode is a retrieval of every
   letter in it (the "self-teaching" effect). So word-only lessons do not pause letter learning: they space it.
   This is the strongest argument for the owner's idea.
2. **Spacing beats massing:** a symbol should come back in new words over the next 5–10 lessons (see 2b). Word
   lessons that deliberately pick words using the thin symbols (◌ູ, ໄ◌, ◌ບ, ◌ມ, ໂ◌) do exactly this.
3. **Load counts meanings too.** Three new words with no new symbol is a light lesson. Six (like L11) is the
   ceiling. Keep word-only lessons to 3 core words plus 1–2 bonus, and at most 2 word-only lessons in a row, so the
   learner still feels progress in the script.
4. **Meaningful context and early wins:** "I don't understand", "I'm full", "I'll pay" and "my mother" are said on
   day one of a visit. Hearing and reading them in a conversation the learner can act out sticks better than a word
   list, and motivates more.
5. **Look-alikes:** word lessons bring their own look-alikes, and each needs a planned contrast (decision 15):
   ສີ່ sii 'four' / ສີ see 'colour' (a tone mark), ນ້ອງ nawng 'younger sibling' / ນ້ອຍ noy 'small' (one final), ແມ່ mae
   'mother' / ແມ່ນ maen 'is' (one final), ໝູ່ muu 'friend' / ໝູ muu 'pork' (keep ໝູ out for now), ເກົ້າ kao 'nine' /
   ເຂົ້າ khao 'rice' / ເຂົ່າ kao 'knee', ໄກ kai 'far' / ໃກ້ kai 'near' (a contrast lesson, as the owner asked).

## 4. Migration plan

**What the schema allows** (`20261019000700_lesson_tables.sql`, `list_lessons` in `20261111020000_activity_data_bonus.sql`):
`lessons.lesson_order` is `integer not null unique`, and a lesson opens when the lesson with `lesson_order - 1` is
done. So an inserted lesson means renumbering every later `lesson_order`. A gap from a retired lesson locks every
lesson after it. Lesson rows upsert on `lesson_id`, so a migration can renumber (in two steps, or the unique
constraint trips). `sections.first/last_lesson_order` must move too. `lesson_tool.py merge` always appends
(`lesson_id = order = n+1`), and the distractor pool is "lessons up to `greatest(order, 5)`", so an insert also
changes which wrong options later lessons may use.
