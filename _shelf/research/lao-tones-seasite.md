## Romanization key used on the site (inferred from examples)
- Tone digits placed after the vowel: 1 = HIGH FALLING, 2 = HIGH RISING, 3 = LOW FALLING, 4 = LOW RISING, # = HIGH MID. (From the tone chart: "(4) LOW RISING", "(2) HIGH RISING", "(3) LOW FALLING", "(1) HIGH FALLING", "(#) HIGH MID".) Some lessons instead use accents (a-acute etc.); inconsistent.
- Capital letters: OO = open o (ɔ), O = same short; EE = ɛ; N = ng; y = ɯ (vowel, "y"/"yy") but also consonant y; ± = palatal ny- (ຍ initial); ? = glottal stop; @ / # etc. appear as artifacts of tone marks.
- "kaaN" = Middle, "su4uN" (also Su4uN) = High, "ta#m" = Low. Those class names are Lao words (sung = high, kaang = middle, tam = low); note the name "ta#m" is written with high-mid tone marker, "su4uN" with low rising.

## 1. Introduction (Introduction_body.htm)
- Origin: Lao script descends from an Indian script, adapted by Cambodians; in the 13th century Thai King Rama Khamheng borrowed and modified it. Page says Lao alphabet is essentially the same as modern Thai, main difference being letter style. [claim stated by source; historical details not verified]
- Six sounds have two Lao letters each: kh, s, th, ph, f, h. Explained as a tone-marking mechanism in a tonal language.
- Three classes:
  - High (su4uN): the first symbol of each of the six duplicated sounds.
  - Low (ta#m): the second symbol of each duplicated sound plus sonorants (ng, y, n, m, r, l, v).
  - Middle (kaaN): k, c, d, t, b, p, y, and the glottal stop.
- A bare consonant letter is pronounced with the vowel "OO" (ວ ເ ອ ໍ sound as name, e.g. "kOO").
- Spelling example: two words with same consonants and short vowel but different tones: High-class initial + sonorant final gives rising tone; Low-class initial in same pattern gives high tone. (No Lao script captured. [UNCERTAIN])
- Recommends the Consonants Poster and Lao Tones chart.
- Front page says the lessons cover letter shapes, three classes and "five spoken tones of Vientiane Lao", tone changes in polysyllabic words, tone-contrast dialogs, using a Lao dictionary.

### Pronunciation notes for English speakers (LaoConsonantSounds, kaang_body, tam_body)
Page gives an "English sound note"/sample table:
- High class: kh = k aspirated (kart); s (saw); th = t aspirated (talk); ph = p aspirated (paw); f (finch); h (hang); ng (song, as in IPA N); y (yam); m (man); n (nancy); l (land); v (van).
- Middle class: k = "g (unaspirated)", IPA k, sample "gas"; j = "j (voiceless)", IPA c, sample "jaw"; t = unaspirated, IPA t, sample "stay"; d (deck); b (boy); p = unaspirated, IPA p, sample "spy"; y (young); aw = glottal stop, IPA ?.
- Low class: kh aspirated k (kart); ng (hang); s (saw); y = "ny", described as like Russian 'nyet' (so ຍ initial is palatal nasal, written ± ); th aspirated; n (no); m (man); ph aspirated; f (finch); h (hang); l (law); v ("v (w final)", van). ວ is v initially and w finally.
- kaang_body: the Middle consonants are not aspirated (no puff of air); IPA k is similar to English "g"; IPA c closer to English "j"; t like the t in "stop"; p like the p in "spot"; t and p voiceless. ອ (?OO) is treated as a consonant and also carries vowels that begin a syllable.
- Final consonants: ບ and ດ are pronounced p and t at the end of a syllable. ຢ is never used as a final; ຍ is final y. Final ວ = w. (Final ຢ/ບ/ດ statements from kaang_body.)
- tam_body notes: ຣ is usually pronounced like ລ; some words are spelled with one, some with the other; ຍ is "y" at end and "±" at start. Example ຍາ (±áa), ຍາຍ (±áay), ກາຍ (kaay).
- Initial ng- : no special tip beyond "ng (song/hang)" in the sound table; no tip on difficulty. [GAP]

## 4. Syllable definitions (suung_body.htm)
- Smooth or Live: syllable ends in a long vowel or a final -m, -n, -N, -l, -r, -y, -w. "Air can continue flowing."
- Checked or Dead: syllable ends in -p, -t, -k, or a glottal stop; airflow stopped by lips, tongue tip/blade, back of tongue or glottis.
- "live" and "dead" are said to derive from Lao terms pen and taay, corresponding to phonetic "smooth" and "checked".
- Tone chart footnotes: "Smooth/Live syllables end in a long vowel or in -m, -n, -ng, -r, -l, -w, -i, or -y." Checked/Dead: "final -p, -t, -k, or a glottal stop (-?)."
- A short vowel with no final (open short syllable) ends in a glottal stop and is treated like a dead syllable for tone (see Low class rule 7; Middle rule 6; High rule 9). The page does not label such syllables "dead" explicitly. [inference flagged]
- Three-question reading method: Is the syllable live or dead? Is the initial High, Middle, or Low? Is there a tone mark? (suung_body; page wording for Q3 relates to Middle/Low.)

### Tone marks
- Page "Tone Marks" says there are four marks: mai ek, mai to, mai tee, mai jattawa. Only ek and to are "used much" (kaang_body). The tonepractice chart shows all four marks on sample syllables (ກ່າ ກ້າ ກ໊າ ກ໋າ), but the page does not say which spoken tone the latter two give. Treat mai tee/jattawa as rare, no rules in source.

### Rules as stated in lessons (with all examples; romanization as given, digits = tone key above)

HIGH class (suung_body.htm). "Answer!" boxes were hidden, so no tone answers for practice items.
1. su4uN + long vowel = low rising. Examples: ຂາ kha4a; ສໍ sO4O; ຖື thyy4; ເຂີ khe4e; ເສ se4e; ຖູ thu4u. Practice (no romanization): ສາ, ເຖ, ແສ, ຖີ, ຂໍ, ຂູ.
2. su4uN + short vowel + sonorant = low rising. Examples: ຜົມ pho4m; ຝົນ fo4n. Practice: ຖານ, ສູງ, ຝັງ, ຜິວ, ສາວ, ຫົງ, ເຂັມ, ຫິນ, ຂາຍ, ຖອຍ. Contrast sets: Low-class ງູ Nu2u, ມໍ mO2O, ຍາ ±áa, ເນ née, ວີ víi, ລື lyy2 versus ຫ-combination (High) ຫງູ Nu4u, ໝໍ mO4O, ຫຍາ ya4a, ເໜ ne4e, ຫວີ vi4i, ຫລື/ຫຼື lyy4.
3. Any consonant + mày eêk = high-mid ("always marks high-mid tone for all three classes"). Examples: ຫົ່ງ ho#N; ຂ່າ kha#a; ສູ່ su#u; ຫວີ່ vii#; ຜ່ານ pha#a.
4. Middle or Low + mày thóo = high falling. Examples: ຕົ້ນ tòn; ບ້າ bàa; ທ້າຍ thàay; ນ້ອງ nOON (printed n O1 ON).
5. High + mày thóo = low falling. Examples: ຂ້ອຍ khO3Oy; ຜູ້ phu3u.
6. Summary: any + ek = high-mid; Middle/Low + to = high falling; High + to = low falling.
7. Dead syllables defined (see sec. 4); examples ສູບ suûp; ຫາກ haâk (tones asked, answers hidden).
8. High + long vowel + stop = low falling. Practice sentences only.
9. High + short vowel = high rising (open short; no examples given on page text).
10. High + short vowel + stop = high rising. Examples: ຫົກ hók; ສິບ síp; ເໜາະ nO2 (printed); ເຜັດ phét; ຝົດ fót; ເໝາະ mO2; ຖັກ thák; ຂຸ khú.
